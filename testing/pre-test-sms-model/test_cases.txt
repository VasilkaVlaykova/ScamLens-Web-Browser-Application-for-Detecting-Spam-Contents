# SMS Model Pre-Testing Results

## Observation

During pre-testing, the SMS model classified several phishing-style SMS messages as Ham, even though the messages contained spam indicators such as parcel delivery pressure, unpaid fees, urgency, payment requests, and suspicious URLs.

## Examples

Input SMS:
UPS: Your parcel delivery has been placed on hold due to an unpaid £1.75 customs fee. Complete payment within 24 hours to avoid returning your parcel:
http://parcel-check-example.test

Amazon Prime: Your membership has been renewed for £89.99. If this was not you, cancel immediately:
http://prime‑cancel‑secure.com

UK Gov: You qualify for a £350 energy support refund. Submit your details before midnight to receive payment:
http://gov‑energy‑claim.uk

UPS: Your parcel is on hold due to an unpaid £1.75 service fee. Complete payment to release delivery:
http://ups‑parcel‑verify.net

Barclays: Your recent payment of £429.99 has been flagged as suspicious. Confirm your identity to avoid account freeze:
http://barclays‑secure‑check.co

Expected Result:
Spam

Actual Result:
Ham

Status:
Fail

## Interpretation

The SMS model may be limited because the SMS dataset is relatively small and may not contain enough modern phishing examples, especially delivery scams and URL-based attacks.

## Next Improvement

The next testing phase will combine the SMS model with the URL model. If the SMS model predicts Ham but a suspicious URL is detected, the final system output should change to Suspicious or Spam.
